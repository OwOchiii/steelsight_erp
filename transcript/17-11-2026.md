## 09:45 Claude

Not from literally any angle. Once a face turns past about 60–70° to the side, one eye and most of the face are hidden, and from behind there's no face at all. ArcFace, the model in `buffalo_l`, was trained mostly on near-frontal faces, so match scores drop steadily as the head turns. No InsightFace setting fixes that.

You can still widen the working angle from roughly ±30° to roughly ±60°, and set things up so the angle matters less. Here's what I'd change, starting with the biggest win:

**1. Store several face photos per worker (biggest gain)**
Right now `ErpGallery` keeps **one vector per worker** (`identity.py:5`). That vector comes from the enrollment photo, which is usually frontal. A face at 45° compared against a frontal-only vector often lands below `FACE_MATCH_THRESHOLD = 0.45`.
- Enroll 3–5 photos per worker: frontal, turned left and right about 30–45°, and slightly up and down.
- Keep all the vectors and score each worker by their best-matching vector.
- `ErpGallery.match` barely changes, since it's already one matrix multiply. The real work is on the ERP side, because `face_embedding` currently allows one READY row per worker.
- A shortcut that needs no schema change is to average the embeddings into one vector. It helps, but less than keeping them separate.

**2. Skip faces that are turned too far**
SCRFD already returns 5 landmarks (`face.kps`), and the head's left-right turn can be estimated from where the nose sits between the eyes. At the moment, any face it finds gets embedded and matched. A side profile scores low and then sets `unknown=True` (`faceid.py`, near the end of `_worker`). That's a false "stranger" flag for a worker who was only looking sideways. I'd do two things:
- Ignore faces turned more than about 45° (or lower their weight).
- Only set `unknown` after several clear frontal looks fail to match.

**3. Check more often before a person is recognized**
`FACE_RECHECK_SECONDS = 1.0` means a person crossing the gate in 2–3 seconds only gets 2–3 tries. If you try every 0.3–0.5 s while a track is still unrecognized, you're much more likely to catch the moment they look toward the camera. Because the design recognizes a person once and then follows them by tracking, losing the face afterwards doesn't matter.

**4. Camera placement (no code, often the biggest real-world factor)**
- Mount the gate camera at roughly head height plus a little, tilted down 10–20°, facing the direction people walk in.
- Put it at a narrow point people must pass through, so they're walking toward it.
- A steep overhead camera puts every face at a bad angle, whatever the model.
- A second camera at a different angle at the same gate also helps.

**5. Bigger model (modest gain)**
`antelopev2` uses a larger ArcFace model (R100) and handles turned faces somewhat better than `buffalo_l`. It's slower, though, and you'd have to re-enroll everyone because the embeddings aren't compatible.

## 10:42 Developer

How does ocr actually work to recognize the number on the vest

## 10:42 Claude

The vest number is read by EasyOCR, an off-the-shelf text reader, in `steelsight/people/vest.py`. It only runs when YOLO has already found a vest on a person. YOLO finds the vest, EasyOCR reads digits inside it, several reads must agree, and the number is then looked up in the vest-to-worker links that the gate camera creates at check-in.

### 1. Find the vest and match it to a person
Every frame, YOLO detects `Vest` boxes and the tracker follows people. `vest_boxes_by_pid()` gives each vest box to the person box that contains the vest's centre. If two people overlap, the smaller person box wins, because it's the closest fit. No vest box means no OCR for that person.

### 2. Crop the vest
`torso_crop()` cuts out the vest box with 8% padding. If the shorter side is under 48 px (`VEST_OCR_MIN_PX`), the crop is skipped because the digits would be too small to read.

### 3. Queue the crop, without slowing the camera
OCR is slow, so the engine never waits for it:
- Each tracked person gets at most one crop waiting in the queue.
- A crop is sent every 0.6 s (`VEST_OCR_RECHECK_SECONDS`). Once a number is confirmed, only every 2.4 s.
- A separate thread processes the queue.

### 4. Clean up the image (`_prep`)
Reflective vests often glare or have low contrast, so before reading:
- small crops are enlarged so the short side is about 192 px (at most 4×)
- the crop is converted to grayscale
- local contrast is boosted (CLAHE), which brings out printed digits on a shiny background

### 5. EasyOCR reads it
EasyOCR runs in two steps. A text-detection model (CRAFT) finds where text is. A recognition model then reads each text region. The code tells it to accept only the characters `0123456789`, so it never reads a letter.

From everything it finds, `read_number()` keeps one number:
- it must be 1–4 digits long (`VEST_NUMBER_MIN/MAX_DIGITS`)
- its confidence must be at least 0.45 (`VEST_OCR_MIN_CONF`)
- it scores each candidate as confidence × text height, so big, clear digits beat small text like a logo or a size label
- leading zeros are dropped, so "012" becomes "12"

### 6. Several reads must agree before a number counts
A single read can be wrong, for example 8 read as 3 or 1 as 7. So each person keeps their last 6 reads, and a number is confirmed only once the same one appears at least twice (`VEST_OCR_CONFIRM_HITS`). A failed read, from a turned body or something in the way, changes nothing. After confirmation it keeps re-reading, less often, in case the tracker swapped two people.

### 7. From number to worker
OCR only gives a number, and the number means nothing on its own. The link to a worker comes from the gate:
- **Entering:** the face identifies the worker, and the vest number read at that moment is linked to them (`LinkBook.link`, synced to the ERP's `ppe_linking`).
- **On site:** the face is often unusable from a distance, so reading the number is enough. `links.worker_for(number)` returns the worker, and violations get recorded under their name.
- **Leaving:** the link is removed, so the vest can go to someone else the next day.

ERP vest codes like `V-012` are matched through `vest_key()`, which keeps only the digits without leading zeros (`12`), the same form OCR produces.

### Where it struggles
- **Angle and folds:** digits on a curved, wrinkled vest seen from the side get misread or missed. Requiring two matching reads hides most of this.
- **Distance:** once the vest is under 48 px tall, it isn't read at all.
- **No vest box, no read:** if YOLO misses the vest, OCR never runs. The fallback in `torso_crop()` that crops the torso from the person box exists, but `update()` only queues a crop when there's a vest box, so it never runs.
- **Similar digits:** 1/7, 3/8 and 6/8 confusions on low-quality frames. Since only 2 of 6 reads have to agree, two identical misreads can confirm a wrong number. If that happens, raise `VEST_OCR_CONFIRM_HITS` to 3.

If you want better accuracy, the usual improvements are bigger, thicker digits printed on both front and back of the vest, and a small digit-recognition model trained on your own vest photos instead of the general-purpose EasyOCR. I can look into either.

1. The worker stands in front of the kiosk and their face is confirmed.
2. If this is a check-in, it waits up to **3 seconds** (`SCANNER_IDENTIFY_SECONDS`) for the OCR to read a vest number.
3. If a number comes through, it links the vest to the worker, just like the gate. If not, it checks the worker in anyway with no vest linked. On the gate screen that row shows "—" in the vest number column.
4. On check-out, it removes the link.
